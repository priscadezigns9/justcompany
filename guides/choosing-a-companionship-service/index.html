Choosing a companionship service for an adult

Questions to ask about suitability, boundaries, location, privacy, expenses, and what happens before a session is confirmed.

If you are looking for companionship for yourself or an adult family member, clear answers can help you decide whether a service is a good fit. Ask about the service’s scope, how requests are reviewed, where sessions take place, what costs cover, and how privacy and boundaries are handled. A careful provider should be able to explain its process without promising that every request can be accepted or that every risk can be removed.

1. What does “companionship” mean here?

Start by asking what the service includes—and what it does not. A managed companionship service may arrange conversation and shared activities, but that does not automatically mean it provides personal care, clinical support, transport, therapy, or emergency help. Make sure the service description matches the kind of support you actually need.

Just Company is a managed, paid, non-medical companionship service. It is not nursing, home care, medical advice, therapy, emergency response, a transport service, or dating. If the need involves health care, personal care, mobility assistance, or urgent help, look for a provider qualified for that specific need.

2. How is a request assessed?

Ask whether you are entering an open directory where people browse profiles, or whether a team reviews requests and coordinates suitable opportunities. Find out whether a request is automatically accepted, whether a companion can decline, and what happens if availability changes.

Just Company reviews each enquiry for suitability and availability. It is not an open marketplace: the team reviews the request privately and contacts you about next steps. Companions also decide whether to accept or decline an opportunity, so a preferred companion or match cannot be guaranteed.

3. Where and when do sessions take place?

Ask which locations, activities, and times are permitted. Check whether home visits are offered, how travel is handled, and whether a venue must be approved before a meeting. These details affect comfort and safety, so they should be clear before anyone makes plans.

At launch, Just Company sessions are limited to selected public community locations or approved facilities, and take place in daylight. Private-home visits are not available. Requests outside the agreed service area need separate review and approval. Each person arranges their own transportation unless a separate written safety arrangement is approved.

4. What does the fee cover?

Ask for a clear explanation of the service fee and whether meals, drinks, tickets, travel, or personal purchases are included. If a companion expense might be covered, find out when that is agreed and whether the companion can decline. Avoid assumptions about direct payments or extra costs.

For Just Company, the service fee covers the companion’s time and service coordination. Travel, meals, drinks, tickets, and personal purchases are optional and are not automatically included. Expense arrangements should be discussed before confirmation; the companion sees them before deciding whether to accept. Current fee and payment details are provided through the enquiry process.

One important distinction

An enquiry is not a confirmed booking or payment. Wait until the service has reviewed suitability and availability, agreed on the details, and explicitly confirmed the session.

5. How are privacy and safety approached?

Ask how identity and screening are handled, what information stays private, and whether there is a public directory or direct contact before a booking is approved. Ask how check-ins and check-outs work, how concerns are reported, and what either person should do if they feel uncomfortable.

Just Company describes its safeguards in its public Safety and Privacy procedure. These include private screening and controlled coordination, selected public locations, agreed boundaries, and check-in/check-out steps. The company is clear that no system can remove every risk; safeguards reduce avoidable risk but cannot guarantee someone’s behaviour or that an incident will not occur.

6. Can either person change their mind?

Ask whether participants can decline an opportunity, adjust an arrangement before confirmation, or end a session if they feel unsafe or uncomfortable. Clear consent and boundaries should continue throughout—not only when the booking is made.

At Just Company, companions may accept or decline each opportunity. Either person may end a session immediately if they feel unsafe, uncomfortable, or pressured. The agreed activity, location, timing, expenses, boundaries, and contact process should be settled before a booking is confirmed.

Choose based on fit, not just availability

Before deciding, compare the service’s scope with the adult’s actual needs, read its terms and safety information, and ask any questions that remain. Do not rely on vague claims such as “fully safe” or assume that screening guarantees a particular outcome. A clear explanation of limits is a sign that the provider is taking the arrangement seriously.

If Just Company’s boundaries and process sound suitable, you can begin with a private enquiry. The team will review the request and contact you about availability and next steps.
